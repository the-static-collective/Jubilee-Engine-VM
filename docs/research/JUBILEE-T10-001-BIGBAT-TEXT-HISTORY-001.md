## 6. Jeremiah 34 — real deror, not secure Jubilee execution

The phrase overlaps Leviticus 25 release vocabulary, but scholarship disputes literary direction and legal relation between Jeremiah 34 and Leviticus 25. The episode does not establish that a scheduled Jubilee year was being observed.

## 7. Ancient Near Eastern release comparanda

Hebrew deror is commonly compared with Akkadian andurārum and related royal release terminology.

Ancient Near Eastern clean-slate edicts could include debt remission, release of debt-servants, and reversal of some alienations.

This supports the historical reality of publicly enacted release as a legal-political operation.

It does not establish identity:

~~~text
Leviticus Jubilee != Babylonian royal clean-slate edict
~~~

Useful contrast:

~~~text
ANE royal edict:
  discretionary sovereign intervention

Levitical Jubilee:
  recurring covenantal / calendrical legislation
~~~

The relation is comparative, not identity.

## 11. 11QMelchizedek — ancient braid; exact 491 reconstruction disputed

11QMelchizedek / 11Q13 is a Second Temple Jewish Hebrew text that eschatologically braids material from Leviticus 25, Deuteronomy 15, Isaiah 61, Day of Atonement, and Danielic chronology.

This shows that a Jubilee + release + atonement + chronology braid existed in Jewish interpretation before Christianity.

The exact chronological reconstruction is less secure. Older readings locate the event around culmination of the tenth Jubilee; newer manuscript work argues for the beginning of the first Jubilee after ten Jubilees, effectively the 491st year.

Therefore:

~~~text
SECURE:
  ancient Jubilee / release / atonement / eschatological braid

NOT FROZEN AS INVARIANT:
  exact 490 -> 491 reconstruction
~~~
